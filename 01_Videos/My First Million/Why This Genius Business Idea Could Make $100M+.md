## Summary

Sam Parr highlights a deceptively simple product business built around locking phones into magnetic pouches. What he finds impressive is not the object itself but the sequencing: start with a celebrity use case, prove the pain is real, then scale into schools where the need is larger, more repeatable, and easier to budget for.

## Key Insights

- The initial wedge worked because performers had a clear and urgent pain around leaked sets.
- The business became much bigger once it shifted from events to institutional buyers.
- Schools are compelling because the emotional and educational benefits are easy to articulate to administrators.
- A plain physical product can still become massive when it sits at the center of a behavioral problem people badly want solved.
