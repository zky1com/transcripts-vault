## Summary

Sam and Shaan unpack the Timeleft story as a case study in persistent iteration rather than overnight genius. What finally worked was not a radically complex product but a sharp reset: Maxime Barbier constrained himself to build something fast, make money quickly, remove the features that pushed the product toward dating dynamics, and deliver one socially compelling ritual that fit the loneliness problem better than his earlier attempts.

## Key Insights

- Timeleft only looks like a fast success if you ignore the three years of failed products that came first.
- The winning version got simpler when Barbier forced himself to use no-code tools and validate revenue quickly.
- The group-dinner format solved the safety and dating confusion that broke earlier one-on-one and photo-based versions.
- The company scaled once he stopped treating local restaurant setup as a sacred manual process.
- The back half of the episode turns into a practical discussion of how to use ChatGPT more like a questioning coach than a search bar.
