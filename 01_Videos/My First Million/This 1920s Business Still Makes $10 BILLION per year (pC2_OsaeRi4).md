## Summary

Sam Parr uses the history of Cintas to show how an unglamorous service business can become a giant when it expands methodically from one recurring operational need into a broader bundle of adjacent necessities. The story is framed as a reminder that huge value often gets built in categories most people ignore because they look too ordinary.

## Key Insights

- The starting point was a tiny, niche laundry service rather than a grand invention.
- The second generation mattered because it pushed the company from a stable trade into deliberate expansion.
- Recurring business-to-business service relationships gave the company room to add more products over time.
- Parr presents the category's boring appearance as part of the opportunity rather than a drawback.
