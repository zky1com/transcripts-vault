## Summary

Sam tells the short Grey Goose origin story as an example of deliberate premium positioning. The key lesson is that a consumer product can look radically more valuable just by changing the country narrative, the package silhouette, and the reference price people compare it against.

## Key Insights

- The founder first proved he could manufacture demand by turning Jägermeister into a party ritual.
- Grey Goose was built on a few intentional signals rather than on obvious product superiority.
- Packaging and pricing were used together to make the vodka feel premium from first glance.

## Claims

- [[Sam Parr - Premium consumer brands can be created by changing the story, the silhouette, and the price anchor all at once]]
