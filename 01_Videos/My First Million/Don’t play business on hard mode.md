## Summary

This short clip argues that business gets easier after your first meaningful win because prior success becomes leverage for the next opportunity. Instead of romanticizing the solo-founder struggle, Shaan frames smart early-career strategy as borrowing someone else's existing advantage and converting it into your own.

## Key Insights

- He treats prior success as a platform, not an endpoint.
- His advice is anti-romantic: if collaboration gives you leverage, use it instead of proving you can suffer alone.
- The clip is fundamentally about compound advantage rather than about tactics.

- [[Shaan Puri - Early success becomes a platform that makes later opportunities easier]]

One of the unfair secrets of business is the more success you have, the more it's like a platform [music] for a bigger thing you can do next time. You just got to keep in the game. When I was really young, I used to think, oh, I can start a business and make a lot of money and then go to the beach and then that would be like fun. That's not nearly as fun as the fact that once you've already started and successful once, now you get to do like bigger things more easily. When you're young, partner with someone else who already has that unfair advantage, help them use their unfair advantage, build something with them. Now you have all these extra unfair advantages, you can do it again. There's like this naive thing where like you're like this lone business founder and you can do that, but it's so hard. >> Why play hard mode? Why play game on hard mode? Every time you have a success, you now have the respect and attention and understanding of all these people who will then help you do the next thing.
