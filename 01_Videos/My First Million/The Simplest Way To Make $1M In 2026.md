## Summary

Sam Parr, Shaan Puri, John Coogan, and Jordi Hays build a fresh version of Sarah's List: companies they would join if the goal were to make a first million through employee equity rather than by starting from scratch. The discussion keeps returning to the same theme from different angles: many people overcomplicate wealth creation, underestimate how far obvious winners can still run, and fail to appreciate how much advantage comes from backing operators with real category depth and durable technical moats.

## Key Insights

- The panel treats joining a proven winner as a legitimate high-upside strategy rather than a lesser version of founding.
- Several of the strongest opportunities come from deep industrial or infrastructure moats, not just flashy software narratives.
- They repeatedly favor proven operators with unusual execution history over abstract market storytelling.
- Obvious winners remain a recurring blind spot because many people assume late entry means limited upside.
- Vertical AI and infrastructure businesses stand out when they attack larger labor or systems budgets rather than small software line items.
