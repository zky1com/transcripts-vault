## Summary

Anne Mahlum explains how she went from founding the nonprofit Back on My Feet to building Solidcore into a nine-figure fitness business. The throughline in both stories is her bias toward direct conviction: she sets high expectations, raises money with concrete asks, moves fast on branding and expansion, and treats emotional intensity as a core part of how people commit to movements and businesses.

## Key Insights

- She built Back on My Feet by refusing to lower standards for people simply because they were struggling.
- Her early fundraising accelerated when she targeted senior decision makers who could write checks and make introductions immediately.
- She sees branding as something that needs to feel cool and be good enough to move, not a place to get stuck perfecting.
- She is bullish on challenge-based fitness experiences because people increasingly want hard physical events that map to deeper personal change.
- She thinks AI will make generic fitness programming look increasingly outdated because personalization will get much better.
