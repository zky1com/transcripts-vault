Watching the bond market is extremely important in this investing environment because bond rates are hitting 20-year highs, and I would argue that nobody is prepared for higher rates. The 10-year Treasury is at 4.6%. The 1-year Treasury is at 4%. Rates are going higher because there is so much government debt refinancing of that debt coming. Higher interest rates to justify the risks. And if I look at the national debt and compare it to the net interest, it's 1 trillion per year. But with the 4% interest rate, just on that debt that's there, as the debt is refinanced, it will go to 1.6 trillion. Then, if we look at the new deficit that's coming year by year, in 5 years, it's very likely that US government debt will be 50 trillion. 50 trillion times 4%, that's 2 billion in interest. It already doubled. Interest payments doubled over the last 5 years. They were below 500 billion, above 1 trillion now, and going to above 2 trillion. That's crazy, and that's a huge risk. And when you look at these numbers, you start also thinking about the risk of investing in stocks because the S&P 500 dividend yield is just 1% at historical lows. It was even higher in the dot-com bubble. What happened afterward? The market crashed 60%, and then you start thinking, if I have a few million, if I put them into Treasuries, 10-year, I get 100,000 per year, no matter what, and then I get my 10 million or few million back. So, no matter what, with Treasuries now, you can't lose. Stocks, there is a chance of a 70% market crash. I would say it will be very interesting times ahead. Watch your risk-reward so that you can reach your financial goals over time.
