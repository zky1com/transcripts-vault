## Summary

Stephen Miran argues it is too early to let the Iran-related oil shock derail his rate-cut path because long-run inflation expectations still look contained and the broader data have not forced a forecast change. He still favors continued gradual cuts toward neutral while watching for evidence that Middle East stress is bleeding into broader inflation or overheating.

## Key Insights

- Miran thinks one stronger print is not enough to dismiss the story of gradually softening labor markets.
- He places more weight on market inflation expectations than on short-run consumer reactions to oil and headline inflation.
- His baseline remains continued 25-basis-point cuts unless incoming data clearly change the inflation or labor outlook.
- He still sees a plausible path to inflation undershooting target if housing and goods disinflation arrive faster than expected.
