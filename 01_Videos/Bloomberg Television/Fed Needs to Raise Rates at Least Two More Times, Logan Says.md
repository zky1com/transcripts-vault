I anticipate that without any monetary
policy restraint, inflation will continue to trend above target unless

target range is at least 50 basis points, half a percentage point below
the level that would be would create modest restriction and begin to bring
the inflation trend back to target. After raising the fed funds target range
by 25 basis points in September. The FOMC skipped an increase at our most
recent meeting. Financial markets had been volatile in
the weeks before the meeting. In response to news on the technology
investment outlook and on geopolitical developments
in such an environment. Adjusting policy more gradually can help
households, businesses and market participants adapt to higher rates and
reduce the risk that economic activity deteriorates more sharply than
necessary. Moreover, as long as market participants
understand the FOMC policy plans, the precise timing of each rate increase has
little effect on the longer term rates that most
borrowers pay. So skipping one meeting does not change
the monetary policy destination. Indeed, by making that destination
clear, the FOMC can better help the economy adapt and obtain the promised
benefits of a skip. It remains clear to me, given the
current outlook, that the FOMC needs to implement at least two more.

It would make sense, in my view, to pause and examine economic and financial
developments to determine what further action may be appropriate.
The goal should be to make voluntary policy modestly restrictive so we can
sustainably deliver both maximum employment and price stability.
Market and survey based measures of long run inflation expectations are near 2%,

by communicating and delivering on a strategy for moving to a modestly
restrictive stance. The fed can continue to earn that trust.
Of course, I'll change my policy views if the economic outlook and risks
