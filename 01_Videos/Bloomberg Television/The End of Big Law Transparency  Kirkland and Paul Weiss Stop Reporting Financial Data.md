Kirkland and Ellis, the world's highest grossing law firm, reported average profits per equity partner of 11.1 million last year. And those are the last numbers that the law firm ever [music] plans to make public. For about 40 years, Kirkland shared its annual financial results with legal publications, which used those figures to rank law firms. The rankings compare law firms by revenue and profits, including profits per equity partner, which is a measure of the average profits generated for each partner with an ownership stake. Those numbers became a measure of prestige in big law. But in early October, Kirkland sent a letter to the American lawyer stating that 2026 would be the last year that it shares its financial results with the media. Bloomberg Law obtained a copy of the letter. We will no longer participate in the rankings process, nor comment on any estimates of our firm's financial results. So why stop after four decades? The one person I talked to who was familiar with the decision said that it had been in the works for years. Kirkland's leaders are wary of how clients are reacting to reports of its equity partner profits. Since 2006, demand for legal services has remained relatively flat, while law firms hourly billing rates have increased $122%.
