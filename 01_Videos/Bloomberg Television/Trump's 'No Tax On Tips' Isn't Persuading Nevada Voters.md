I'm here in Las Vegas where three competitive House seats could be key to determining control of the chamber and the balance of power in Washington for the second half of President Trump's second term. The three seats are currently held by Democrats with Republicans razor-thin majority in the chamber. Flipping those districts could lend the party some breathing room while defending them may be necessary for Democrats' quest to retake control of the house. In 2024, Trump became the first Republican presidential candidate in two decades to carry Nevada. Trump promised to exempt tips from taxation. Now, Republicans are leaning into the new deduction for tips along with tax breaks for overtime and seniors they enacted in last year's one big beautiful bill to boost their chances in the state. Republicans see Nevada's third district represented by Democrat Susie Lee as a key pick up opportunity. Lee is one of just 13 Democrats representing a district Trump won in 2024. The president has endorsed Marty O'Donnell, a video game composer who has already poured 3 million of his own money into his campaign. O'Donnell said he plans to hit Lee hard on her vote against no tax on tips in the big beautiful bill while the congresswoman has made the cost of living and affordability central to her pitch for voters. Response here in Vegas has been mixed. Some we've talked to said the new tax breaks made a big difference on their tax bills lowering what they owed and in some cases still delivering big refunds. But many also said higher costs for food, housing, health care, and now gas have eaten into their savings. Some also said other Trump administration policies had hurt them financially. >> Myself, a lot of my co-workers, we made less last year than we did in 2024. >> Do you remember by how much you were down? >> I was down about 10 grand. >> The Las Vegas Convention and Visitors Authority estimates Canadian visits to the city fell about 17% last year while overall visits dropped 7 and 1/2% the sharpest decline since the pandemic. Many we talked to also worry that the spike in gas prices caused by the war in Iran will deter more visitors as it drives up the cost of travel. Candidates from both parties agree the election will be a referendum on the economy and whether Trump has delivered on his promises to bring prices down and wages up. Here in Southern Nevada, early signs indicate Trump's other policies have blunted the impact of the new tax cuts.
