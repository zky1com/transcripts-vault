As Africa's richest city battles, worsening water shortages. And with municipal elections fast approaching, water has become a defining political issue. I spoke with Helen Zille, former Cape Town mayor and now a candidate for Johannesburg's top job. She says fixing the city's failing water infrastructure would be her first priority. The city cannot get the water to the residents because the infrastructure is so broken. The dams are broken. The city loses 46% of its water to leaks, to broken infrastructure and to theft of water. Those are the major things. And of course, when almost all of your water is lost, there is a massive supply problem. Also, Rand Water has an agreement and there's certain amounts that they can extract from the dams. But in a time of crisis like this, when the dams were overflowing, supply should not be a problem. I think in the longer term, supply will be a problem. I think the Lesotho Highlands project was developed with a view to the extraordinary growth and primary urbanisation that we have in South Africa and then to the economic capital of South Africa and to Gauteng generally. So I think that is to secure the supply for the future. But the current crisis is not a supply crisis, it's a maintenance crisis. So where do you get at in terms of addressing the issue? I mean, there are some stats that say this is a multi-billion dollar deficit for infrastructure financing that we're looking at for water. Where do you think that the progress starts to come from? Is it like the Lesotho Highlands dam project? No, I don't think so, because even if we got all the water that we wanted from that Lesotho Highlands Dam project, we would still be losing water in the system because of broken pipes and bad maintenance. Now, obviously we fully support the Lesotho Highlands Water Project because it is critical for the future, but we have to start on ground level and fix the infrastructure that we've got. It is a multi billion dollar project because the maintenance has been neglected for 30 years. So for example, if critically endangered reticulation systems in Johannesburg and no matter how much water there is in the system, those reticulation networks can't deliver the water to the people. And that is the crisis currently that we are facing. So Johannesburg's part of resolving the crisis is not at source, not at supply. In any case, that's a national government competence. That's why the agreement is between Lesotho and our national government. But with us in Jo'burg, the problem is with getting the reticulation system in top shape.
