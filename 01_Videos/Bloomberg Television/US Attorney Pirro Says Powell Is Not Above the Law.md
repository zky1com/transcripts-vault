## Summary

Jeanine Pirro argues that a district judge improperly blocked her office from using grand jury subpoenas to investigate Jerome Powell and the Federal Reserve over renovation cost overruns. She says her office tried repeatedly to engage voluntarily before issuing subpoenas, that the subpoenas were directed at the Fed rather than Powell personally, and that the judge's intervention wrongly insulated Powell from ordinary investigative scrutiny. The statement is fundamentally a prosecutorial accountability argument rather than a monetary-policy note.

## Key Insights

- Pirro says the case is about whether investigators can even obtain records, not about proving charges yet.
- She frames grand jury subpoenas as a basic prosecutorial tool that was wrongly taken away.
- She says her office began gathering information months before issuing subpoenas.
- She says the office repeatedly asked the Fed for a meeting or phone call and was ignored.
- She stresses the subpoenas were issued to the Federal Reserve, not directly to Powell.
- She accuses Powell of falsely presenting himself as a threatened victim rather than cooperating.
- She says Judge Boasberg's decision blocked both record review and presentation to the grand jury.
- She ends by insisting Powell is not above the law.
