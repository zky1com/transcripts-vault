Meta is rebooting its virtual reality ambitions in a big way with a new 1299 VR device that looks and feels more like a pair of glasses than a traditional headset. And it could reset the entire VR and mixed reality category. Today's leading devices, including the MetaQuest and the Apple Vision Pro, pack most of their tech into the headset itself, displays, processors, and other essential components. The Quest also houses its battery inside the headset. The Vision Pro's battery is external, but hardware, such as its two built-in chips and cooling fan, still make the device relatively bulky and heavy. That weight can cause neck strain, and for many people, make these devices difficult to use for more than half an hour at a time. But Meta's new VR glasses take a different approach. They move both the battery and the computing hardware into an external puck that's roughly the size of the Vision Pros battery pack. The result is a pair of glasses that feels lighter, more comfortable, and much easier to wear. During my demo, the experience was smooth, responsive, and surprisingly natural. The glasses use several familiar Vision Pro style gestures. Pinch your thumb and index finger to select something, move your hands apart to zoom in on a web page, or swipe to scroll. But one standout feature was a VR keyboard projected onto a tabletop. I was able to type on it almost as easily as I could type on a laptop keyboard. and in practice, it felt genuinely sci-fi. The glasses are planned to go on sale next spring for less than half the price of the Vision Pro. But the best thing Meta may have announced could be its VR avatars. They're extremely lifelike, represent your entire body, and work across both the headset and Ray-B band display glasses. They make virtual conversations feel much more like being in the same room. Meta is also expanding its lineup of glasses without screens. That includes the introduction of third generation Ray-B bands with slightly longer battery life and slimmer frames alongside new camera-free models, including the iconic Ray-B band Club Master style. Those camerafree options arrive amid growing concerns about people using smart glasses for surveillance.
