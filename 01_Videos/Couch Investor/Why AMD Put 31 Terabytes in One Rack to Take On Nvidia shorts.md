Nvidia sells the most wanted computer in AI. AMD just built a rival with 50% more memory and the chip isn't the point. AMD stopped selling chips. So why put 31 TB in one rack? Here's the shift. Big AI buyers don't shop for single GPUs anymore. They buy racks. 72 GPUs wired together so they act like one giant machine. Whoever sells the rack owns the customer. That's Nvidia's moat. Its Vera Rubin NVL72 rack ties 72 GPUs together with NVLink, a private highway between the chips with CUDA software on top. You buy the whole package from Nvidia. Helios is AMD's answer, a whole rack, also with 72 GPUs called the MI455X. Each one carries up to 432 GB of HBM4. HBM4 is the newest high bandwidth memory stacked right next to the chip, so data moves fast. Add it up and one Helios rack holds 31 terabytes versus 20.7 in Nvidia's rack. Think of it as a memory tank against a compute engine. More memory means bigger models and longer context fit inside one rack. Both racks move data between their GPUs at 260 terabytes per second, but Nvidia's engine is stronger on raw speed. On FP4, a compact 4-bit number format AI uses to run models fast. Nvidia's rack hits 3.6 6 Exaflops versus 2.9 for Helios. AMD is betting the tank matters more. The second bet is open versus closed. Envy link belongs to Nvidia. Helios is built on open standards. And the buyers are showing up. AMD says Antropic, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure are already running workloads on the first Helios racks.
