## Summary

In this guest sermon, Candita Hinds uses the metaphor of expecting a hamburger from a hardware store to argue that much emotional pain comes from unrealistic expectations placed on ourselves, other people, and life outcomes. She blends psychoeducational framing with pastoral application, showing how unmet expectations fuel resentment, burnout, and physical stress, then redirects listeners toward clearer communication, self-compassion, therapy, and expectations anchored in God rather than in people or possessions.

## Key Insights

- Unrealistic expectations often come from old wounds, cultural conditioning, fear, or poor boundaries.
- People and systems cannot provide what they do not actually possess.
- Burnout can become a bodily consequence of carrying impossible expectations alone.
- Healing requires both practical reframing and spiritual reorientation toward God.

## Claims

- [[Candita Hinds - People cannot give what they do not have to offer]]
- [[Candita Hinds - Money and external success do not create lasting peace]]
- [[Candita Hinds - Carrying everything alone leads to burnout and physical harm]]
- [[Candita Hinds - Expectations should be anchored in God rather than in people]]
