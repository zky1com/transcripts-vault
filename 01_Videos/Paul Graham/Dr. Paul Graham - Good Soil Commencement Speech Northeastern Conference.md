## Summary

In this commencement speech, Paul Graham reworks the parable of the sower into a message about opportunity, discipline, peer influence, and parental sacrifice. He tells students that education has prepared their ground, warns that opportunity can be ignored, neglected, or choked by the wrong company, and calls them to become good soil that receives, develops, and multiplies what God and their families have invested in them.

## Key Insights

- Graham turns seed into a metaphor for opportunity and insists that receiving opportunity well is a moral choice.
- The speech warns that even strong beginnings can fail when students neglect development or stay around the wrong influences.
- Parental sacrifice is framed as deliberate cultivation rather than mere tuition payment.
- The central challenge is not background but whether students choose to become good soil.
