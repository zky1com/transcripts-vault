## Summary

On day nineteen, Paul Graham turns to John 19 and frames the crucifixion as the moment where Jesus refuses to use divine power for self-preservation so that others can be saved. He emphasizes Pilate's inability to find real fault in Jesus, the crowd's demand for Barabbas instead, and the repeated human tendency to choose against Christ in order to keep sin, control, or comfort.

## Key Insights

- Jesus does not lack power at the cross; he withholds its use for the sake of salvation.
- The choice between Jesus and Barabbas becomes a continuing pattern in human moral life.
- Earthly power is shown to be weak compared with the Father's larger purpose.
- The cross exposes both human hostility and divine mercy at the same time.
