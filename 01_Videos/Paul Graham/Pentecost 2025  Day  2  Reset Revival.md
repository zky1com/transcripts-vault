## Summary

On day two, Paul Graham uses the wedding at Cana to argue that Jesus is concerned with the ordinary pressures and embarrassments people carry, not only their public crises. He presents the empty water jars as a picture of spiritually hollow lives, stresses that obedience is the key to being filled and changed, and frames Christ's miracle as proof that he can turn what is ordinary, empty, and insufficient into something chosen and purposeful.

## Key Insights

- The sermon insists that what matters to people still matters to Jesus, even when it seems small.
- Paul treats the jars as a symbol of lives that have capacity but remain spiritually empty.
- Obedience is presented as the hinge between emptiness and transformation.
- The transformed wine becomes an image of a changed life that Christ presents as choice rather than common.
