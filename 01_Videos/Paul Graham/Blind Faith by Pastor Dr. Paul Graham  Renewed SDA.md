## Summary

In this sermon, Paul Graham uses the pilot-and-instruments analogy, the chair illustration, and Matthew 8's stories of the leper and the centurion to define blind faith as trusting Christ beyond visible proof or familiar routine. He argues that church tradition and manuals must not outrank Scripture, insists that Jesus moves toward sinners and outcasts instead of away from them, and closes by calling listeners to baptismal commitment even when following Christ brings sacrifice and trouble.

## Key Insights

- Faith is deeper than repeating habits that have become familiar or traditional.
- Jesus' authority comes from the word of God rather than from inherited church culture.
- Grace should shape how the church treats people who are visibly struggling.
- Responding to Christ may cost comfort, reputation, or stability, but it is still worth trusting him.
