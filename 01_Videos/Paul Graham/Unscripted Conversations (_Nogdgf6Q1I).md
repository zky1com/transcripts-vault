## Summary

This Unscripted episode begins with a debate over a sermon claiming fornication has been misdefined by the church, then expands into a broader conversation about shame, restoration, and what churches should actually do when people disclose sexual or survival-based harm. The panel rejects shame-driven policing, argues for clearer biblical reading alongside trauma-informed care, and repeatedly says churches need listening, referral, and ethical support structures rather than public judgment.

## Key Insights

- The episode distinguishes between the church's responsibility to teach and its temptation to shame or overreach.
- Several speakers insist that visible sexual sin gets treated more harshly than hidden abuse, infidelity, or exploitation.
- Trauma-informed care and referral emerge as a major corrective to undertrained pastoral responses.
- The panel keeps returning to restoration, safety, and practical support instead of spectacle or condemnation.

## Claims

- [[Shaday Darlington - Churches often over-police visible sexual sin while ignoring other serious moral failures]]

- [[Terry Parks - Churches need listening ministries more than reflexive fixing and judgment]]
- [[Leah Chapman - Pastors should warm-handoff trauma-heavy cases instead of pretending to be fully equipped]]
