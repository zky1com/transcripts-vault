## Summary

Kristalina Georgieva says the IMF is shifting from a small planned global-growth upgrade to a downside scenario exercise because the Iran conflict is creating a worldwide but uneven supply shock. She argues that poorer energy importers with weak reserves are most exposed, while the U.S. and countries with stronger buffers are more protected, and says the IMF may need to mobilize an additional $20 billion to $50 billion in financing support for vulnerable countries.

## Key Insights

- Georgieva says the IMF had only been planning a very small upgrade before the war and is now preparing multiple downside scenarios instead.
- She stresses that vulnerability depends heavily on whether a country is an importer, its reserve position, and the protections it has already built.
- She expects additional IMF financing demand from countries already under strain, especially across parts of Asia, the Middle East, Latin America, and Africa.
