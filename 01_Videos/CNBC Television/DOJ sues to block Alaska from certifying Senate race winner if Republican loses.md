The Department of Justice has filed a highly unusual lawsuit against the Alaska elections officials. They are seeking, the Department of Justice is, to block certification of the Alaska Senate race if the Republican candidate in that race, Daniel Sullivan, were to lose the race. The argument here from the Department of Justice is that the election should not be certified if the incumbent Republican Senator Sullivan loses the race because there is another candidate in the race with a very similar name, Daniel J. Sullivan, is also running for Senate in this race. The Department of Justice here under President Trump is arguing that that is election trickery in essence and designed to confuse voters by running a candidate with a similar name and siphon off Daniel Sullivan, incumbent Senator's, Daniel Sullivan's support. What's interesting about this case, if we can bring up the last paragraph, what the Department of Justice is seeking here is to block the defendants, that is, the Alaska elections officials, from certifying any result that may be impacted by voter confusion pending an investigation to determine whether votes cast for Daniel J. Sullivan were meant to be cast for Senator Sullivan. So, the question is, how can you have an investigation about what voters intent was when they cast their ballot given that votes are anonymous in the United States of America? Seems very difficult to have an investigation of voter intent after the fact. And guys, very striking to see that US Department of Justice filing this case. There used to be a norm around political actions by the Department of Justice. There was a 60-day rule, it was sort of a rule of thumb that the Department of Justice would not file any cases within 60 days of an election that might impact one side or the other of the election. Those days are obviously long since past. We are seeing this just ahead of the election. And of course, what's fascinating here is it's the Trump Department of Justice here weighing in on behalf of the Republican incumbent senator here. Typically, this is the kind of case that would have been filed by the Republican campaign itself saying, "Hey, wait a second. There's trickery in this election. We're being disadvantaged by this. We want to file this case." Here you have the US government stepping in on behalf of the incumbent Republican senator. So, some wild stuff all the way around, guys. Back over to you.
