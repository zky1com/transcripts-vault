## Summary

Tana McKiel looks at a crypto market that remained soft even as hopes for U.S.-Iran talks briefly helped risk assets, with Franklin Templeton's Max Gawkman arguing that digital assets are showing a new kind of ballast because institutional adoption is changing their behavior. He says Bitcoin still fits the digital-gold idea, but more as one piece of a diversified safe-haven basket than as a standalone refuge, and he warns that any U.S. regulatory compromise on stablecoin yields needs to preserve a real benefit for investors.

## Key Insights

- Gawkman argues that institutional participation is changing how crypto behaves during geopolitical stress.
- He treats Bitcoin as a network asset whose value depends on collective belief, much like gold.
- The show frames stablecoin-yield language as the decisive unresolved issue in market-structure talks.
- Tether's move toward a full audit is presented as a notable transparency milestone in the stablecoin race.
