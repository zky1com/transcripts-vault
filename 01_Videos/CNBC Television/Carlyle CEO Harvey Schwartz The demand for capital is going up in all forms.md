## Summary

Harvey Schwartz says Carlyle's stronger medium-term targets reflect a three-year overhaul of the firm's strategy, operating model, compensation design, capital allocation, and leadership structure rather than some one-off cyclical lift. He argues that day-to-day market anxiety around private markets does not change the bigger structural fact that global demand for capital is rising across economies, and he says that demand, not short-term sentiment, is the real long-term driver for firms like Carlyle.

## Key Insights

- Schwartz frames Carlyle's outlook as the product of internal transformation rather than favorable market luck.
- He distinguishes clearly between noisy information flows and what Carlyle sees in underlying portfolio-company activity.
- He argues private capital is becoming more important because governments have less fiscal room to fund growth priorities directly.
- The interview positions Carlyle as a beneficiary of global competition for capital tied to security, growth, and reindustrialization.
