## Summary

Adam Selipsky says economic uncertainty is producing mixed behavior across AWS customers, but he argues that downturns often make the cloud more attractive because it offers cost savings, flexibility, and a way to keep innovating with fewer resources. He also says AWS uses its scale to buffer customers from inflationary swings, intends to keep passing cost efficiencies through via lower prices over time, and feels well staffed enough to pause hiring while watching customer usage and demand signals.

## Key Insights

- Selipsky frames weaker macro conditions as both a spending headwind and a reason some customers accelerate cloud adoption.
- He presents AWS scale as a strategic moat that helps absorb energy and infrastructure volatility on behalf of customers.
- The company is taking a hiring pause but not describing it as a retreat from long-term investment.
- He keeps returning to customer demand signals rather than calendar-based milestones as the trigger for future staffing and investment decisions.
