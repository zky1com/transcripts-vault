## Summary

Dan Greenhaus says investors are being too relaxed about the downside risks embedded in the current Middle East setup, because even without a dramatic new escalation the longer the Strait of Hormuz disruption persists the more oil markets must mathematically embed a larger risk premium. He argues Brent at $130 or $140 is not impossible, thinks investors are defaulting to a familiar buy-the-dip mentality built on years of quick recoveries, and says the danger is especially high because the market was already entering this episode with other stresses, including private-credit worries.

## Key Insights

- Greenhaus is less focused on forecasting the exact military path than on highlighting how asymmetric the market risk is.
- He treats sustained missing barrels as enough to push oil materially higher even without a dramatic new attack.
- His market critique is behavioral: investors have been conditioned to expect every exogenous shock to fade quickly.
- He also emphasizes that this shock is landing on top of an already fragile market backdrop rather than a clean slate.
