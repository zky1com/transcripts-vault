## Summary

Amar Singh explains that the oil market is pricing in a mix of shipping disruption and geopolitical escalation risk tied to the U.S.-Iran conflict, with current Brent pricing sitting between Barclays' most optimistic and more worrisome Strait of Hormuz scenarios. He says the Brent-WTI spread now reflects both higher offshore shipping costs and market concern about possible U.S. export restrictions, and he warns that any attack on upstream or terminal infrastructure could meaningfully extend the duration and severity of the price shock.

## Key Insights

- Singh breaks the Brent-WTI spread into onshore and offshore components, which lets him separate shipping-cost effects from export-policy fears.
- He frames current market pricing as a middle ground between fast normalization and a more prolonged disruption scenario.
- His analysis emphasizes duration risk more than a single headline shock, especially around the Strait of Hormuz.
- He also links LNG stress back to the 2022 energy shock and argues that renewed supply damage could revive coal substitution behavior.
