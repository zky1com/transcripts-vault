## Summary

Leslie Picker reports that Blue Owl faced unusually large redemption requests in two non-traded private-credit funds and responded by capping withdrawals at the standard 5% quarterly limit. She says the requests were far above peers, ties some of the pressure to AI-related worries around software credit exposure, and explains that hedge funds are now offering discounted liquidity to locked-up investors who do not want to wait for redemptions to normalize.

## Key Insights

- The segment frames Blue Owl's issue as a mix of sentiment shock, liquidity design limits, and sector-specific concern around software credit exposure.
- Picker emphasizes that gross inflows softened the economic damage even though headline redemption demand looked extreme.
- The discussion highlights how secondary liquidity providers can exploit gated structures during panic periods.
- The piece also underscores the difference between legal fund terms and what retail investors expect from a product labeled as accessible.
