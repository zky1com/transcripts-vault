Every year I come to the NATO summit, and every year it seems that the current one is the most pivotal yet. From Vilnius in 2023, Washington D.C. in 2024, and last year's key gathering in The Hague, I've had a ringside seat as the same central topic rears its head. And once again, we're set to spend the next 72 hours discussing the same question. Is the US set to walk out of the door and leave Europe's defense to the Europeans? And yet, 12 months ago, the issue seemed to be settled for good as most NATO members agreed with Donald Trump's proposal to increase overall defense spending to 5% including 1.5% on infrastructure expenditure. So, why are we back in the same situation we've been in on and off for 8 years where the US is bemoaning the lack of European action on meaningful spending. By the way, I say 8 years as for me, the lambasting of Jens Stoltenberg, the then NATO SecGen in July 2018 by Trump during his first presidency, was absolutely pivotal. A moment where the US understandably criticized German Chancellor Merkel and Germany especially for wanting cheap Russian oil and gas and US defense from the Russians. So, here we are in Ankara. Mark Rutte and his European allies will spend 2 days trying to convince the US president that Europe is doing enough with at least 95 billion dollars extra spending in defense over the last 12 months and that there is a cohesive route to what is being called burden shifting whereby Europe takes over the heavy lifting for its defense from the US. In addition, though, mutual distrust remains high with Europe feeling bruised over the Greenland debacle and the US feeling aggrieved about a perceived lack of support from NATO allies during Epic Fury and the war with Iran. Of course, the Ukraine war will also be a hot topic of conversation with President Zelenskyy hoping for more NATO and especially US support in the struggle against Russia. All in all, we're set for another fraught episode in the NATO drama with Europe and Canada desperate to keep President Trump on side.
