## Summary

Aaron Levie says investors are beginning to sort software companies by how useful they will actually be in an agentic enterprise future rather than selling the whole sector indiscriminately. He argues that AI agents will become the biggest users of software, that they will still need core enterprise systems like Box to access unstructured data and execute workflows, and that companies should keep their data layer separate from any single model provider so they can swap AI systems as requirements and risks change.

## Key Insights

- Levie sees the software selloff evolving from a blunt rotation into a more nuanced evaluation of which tools agents will actually need.
- He treats Box less as an end-user app and more as a neutral operating layer for enterprise data used by agents.
- His response to the Pentagon-Anthropic issue is to double down on model choice rather than commit to one provider.
- The core strategic message is that in an agentic world, data layers and workflow infrastructure matter as much as the models themselves.
