A new congressional report finds that the digital investment platform Webull, which is headquartered in St. Petersburg, Florida and has 28 million global users, is quietly, quote, tied in structural ways to the government of the People's Republic of China, representing a national security threat to American finance. CNBC has obtained the new 25-page report exclusively from the bipartisan select committee overseeing US-China relations, which finds a profound gap between the firm's public marketing as an American company and the actual control of the firm. In truth, Webull's ownership architecture, technical workforce, technology infrastructure, cross-border data routing, corporate financing, and compliance frameworks are tied in structural ways to the People's Republic of China, the committee found. And it asserted that national security concerns around the company have escalated since October 2025, when Webull began carrying customer cash directly, which the committee alleges creates a structural exposure of billions of dollars in American capital. Webull, which trades on the Nasdaq stock exchange, says it operates a global network of licensed brokerages offering investment services in 18 markets, and the company says it provides retail and institutional investors with 24/7 access to global financial markets. Users of the platform can trade global stocks, ETFs, options, futures, fractional shares, and digital assets on the service. But the congressional committee, which is formally known as the Select Committee on the Strategic Competition between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party, says the online brokerage represents a national security risk because critical back-end systems, personnel, and data flows may remain exposed to the Chinese Communist Party's mandatory intelligence laws and coercive demands. And the report is emerging now uh from under embargo and CNBC will reach out to Webull for its comment on the report uh immediately.
