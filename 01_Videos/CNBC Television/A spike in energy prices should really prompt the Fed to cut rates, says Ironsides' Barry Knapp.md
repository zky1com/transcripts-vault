## Summary

Barry Knapp argues that a sustained energy-price spike would hurt demand more than it would trigger a lasting inflation spiral, so the Federal Reserve should respond by cutting rates rather than tightening. He also says the shale-era U.S. has changed the dollar-oil relationship, making simultaneous dollar and oil strength especially painful for major energy importers like Europe, Japan, and Korea.

## Key Insights

- Knapp treats the current oil move as a demand shock layered onto an economy that was already slowing rather than as classic overheating inflation.
- He argues the U.S. energy position has flipped the old negative correlation between the dollar and oil.
- In his framework, Europe and Asia face the hardest hit because they import energy while paying for it in a stronger dollar.
- He still sees a steeper yield curve and additional Fed cuts as necessary to revive lending and capital investment.
