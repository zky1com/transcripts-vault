## Summary

Roger Ferguson says the latest payroll report reinforces the Fed's view that the labor market is stabilizing rather than deteriorating. He argues that this gives policymakers room to stay focused on both sides of the dual mandate, expects private credit stress to reprice rather than choke off lending entirely, and warns that the longer-run economic risk from the Iran conflict lies in disruptions to the Strait of Hormuz and the inflation expectations that could follow.

## Key Insights

- Ferguson reads the jobs report as confirmation that the labor market is holding steady rather than sliding into weakness.
- He thinks private credit stress is more likely to raise spreads and reprice lending than to cause a major macro credit crunch.
- He sees the war's biggest macro tail risk in energy, shipping, and inflation-expectation spillovers rather than just the immediate headline shock.
