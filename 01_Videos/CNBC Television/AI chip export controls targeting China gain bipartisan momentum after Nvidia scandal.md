Jensen Huang's reported affair with an alleged Chinese spy has increased pressure on Congress to protect American AI dominance. Amid the Nvidia scandal, three export control bills are gaining momentum on Capitol Hill. The bills previously stalled, but are now seeing bipartisan support. One or all of the proposals could be attached to the National Defense Authorization Act. The NDAA authorizes defense funding and is considered must-pass legislation, but has met some resistance from Democrats this year amid concerns over the Iran war. As negotiations play out on the Hill, these are the bills I'm watching. The AI Overwatch Act would require Congress to review the sale of advanced AI chips to American adversaries like China and Iran. It's backed by an unlikely duo: Massachusetts Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren, and Indiana Republican Senator, Jim Banks. Both urged Congress to pass the legislation after the Nvidia scandal broke. They also called on the Commerce Department to close export control loopholes and ensure that advanced AI chips aren't going to China's military. Then there's the Chip Security Act. It would require the Department of Commerce to verify the location of advanced AI chips and ensure they don't end up in unauthorized regions. Finally, the Match act would block the sale of semiconductor manufacturing equipment to adversary countries. The bill advanced out of the House Foreign Affairs Committee in April, but it never got a vote on the House floor. Florida Conservative Representative Brian Mast, who leads the committee, told reporters that similar proposals have gained momentum lately. He said the Trump administration gave marching orders to stop advanced AI chips from being smuggled into China. That's a departure from December 2025, when Trump authorized the sale of Nvidia's H 200 chips to approved customers in China. Before he resigned, former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang lobbied Congress and the Trump administration against banning chip sales to China. He'll now appear before Congress on December 1st, and some Democrats have said they'll conduct probes into him next term. Over the next few weeks, China hawks and the tech industry will be watching to see. Will this Congress make export controls one of its final acts?
