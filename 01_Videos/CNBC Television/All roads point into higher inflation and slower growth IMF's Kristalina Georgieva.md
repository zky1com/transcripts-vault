## Summary

Kristalina Georgieva says the Iran conflict now points the global economy toward higher inflation and slower growth because it functions as a negative supply shock. She argues that the severity depends on whether the ceasefire holds and how much infrastructure damage has already been done, with longer disruption more likely to force central-bank intervention and to hit poorer oil-importing countries hardest.

## Key Insights

- Georgieva sees the conflict primarily as a supply shock that worsens inflation and growth simultaneously.
- She says central banks may be able to look through a short disruption but not a long one that pushes inflation into danger territory.
- She emphasizes that the shock is global but highly asymmetric, with poor oil importers facing the most strain.
