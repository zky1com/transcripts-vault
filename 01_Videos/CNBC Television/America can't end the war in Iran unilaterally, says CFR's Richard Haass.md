## Summary

Richard Haass argues that the United States has reached diminishing military returns in Iran and cannot end the conflict unilaterally because Israel and Iran still have independent choices. He also says the core nuclear issue remains unresolved and ultimately can only be contained through negotiation, not through hopes for regime change or faith that force alone will settle the problem.

## Key Insights

- Haass separates the debate over whether the war should have started from the urgent problem of how it can be ended.
- He treats military action as capable of imposing costs but insufficient for durable nuclear resolution.
- In his view, the conflict persists because other actors retain agency even if Washington wants to stop.
- He sees negotiation as unavoidable, not idealistic, because no other serious path caps both behavior and capabilities.
