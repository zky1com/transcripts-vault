## Summary

Brent Sadler argues that Anthropic has little standing to impose operational limits on how the Pentagon uses its models once those tools are already serving national-security purposes. He treats the bigger issue as the Defense Production Act and the precedent around corporate leverage inside defense relationships, while also acknowledging that AI systems can create security risks because of how they evolve with access to sensitive data.

## Key Insights

- Sadler sees this primarily as a state-power and procurement issue rather than as a moral-rights issue for the AI vendor.
- He argues that liability for misuse should sit with the Pentagon, not with Anthropic, once the military is operating the system.
- He still acknowledges that AI systems can become risky as they absorb sensitive data and continue learning.
- The broader concern for him is precedent around corporate control over defense operations after entering a relationship with the government.
