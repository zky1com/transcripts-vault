## Summary

Peter Boockvar argues that higher oil prices are already forcing the bond market to rethink the likelihood of Fed easing, making it harder for policymakers to respond if expensive energy starts hurting growth. He also says private-credit weakness is broader than just software, with lower-quality credits especially vulnerable if energy costs undermine the economy, while Andrew Slimmon contends that a stronger dollar may help redirect money back into large U.S. stocks rather than chasing energy outright.

## Key Insights

- Boockvar frames the oil shock as both an inflation problem and a policy-constraint problem for the Fed.
- He sees private-credit risk as a broad lower-quality credit issue, not a single-industry story.
- Slimmon is more focused on the stronger dollar and on selective dislocations in U.S. equities than on chasing the commodity move.
- The panel shows how the same oil shock can push investors toward very different asset-allocation responses.
