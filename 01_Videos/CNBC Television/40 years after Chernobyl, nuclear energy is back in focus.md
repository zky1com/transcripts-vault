This is Power Insider. I'm Brian Sullivan and we wanted to highlight a major moment that really changed energy and really a region forever. 40 years ago, the world watched the worst nuclear disaster in history unfold. On April 26th, 1986, a reactor at the Chernobyl nuclear power plant in then Soviet Ukraine exploded during a failed safety test that spewed radioactive material across Europe, forcing mass evacuations and leaving a human and environmental toll that still lingers today. The images, you've seen them, they were chilling. The damage went far beyond the blast zone. Chernobyl really didn't just melt down a reactor. It melted down much of the world's trust in nuclear energy. And it was not just an isolated incident because just 7 years earlier, the partial meltdown at 3M Island in Pennsylvania had already rattled the nuclear industry here in the United States. Those two events put nuclear power really on ice, stalling new projects, sparking global fear and leaving a reputational scar that lasted for decades. But a lot has changed over the last 40 years. Regulators, they're tougher designs, they are smarter. Modern reactors are built around what they call passive safety systems. That means they can shut down without human intervention or even power. And that is not all. A new wave of what they call small module reactors or SMRs for short hope to lower costs and lessen risk. Countries are ramping up investment in nuclear as companies like Microsoft and Amazon, Google, Meta, and more are eyeing it to power their massive data centers around AI. And folks, Wall Street and investors, they are paying attention. Nuclear related stocks like Constellation Energy, Camo, Genova, Oaklo, and more, they have surged over the last year. So, 40 years after Chernobyl, the industry still carries the weight of its past, but it appears to have learned its lessons and is hoping to power a nuclear future.
