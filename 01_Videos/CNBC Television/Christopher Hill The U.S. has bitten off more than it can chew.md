## Summary

Christopher Hill argues that the U.S. response to Iran is unfolding without a sufficiently clear public strategy and at a time when Washington is already overloaded by other major foreign-policy demands. He says there is deep hostility toward the Iranian regime but little evidence yet of a stable successor, warns that striking regional neighbors may be a misstep that only solidifies opposition to Tehran, and suggests the administration is stretching itself too thin across Iran, Ukraine, and China without the diplomatic communication capacity to manage all three well.

## Key Insights

- Hill emphasizes process and state capacity as much as ideology, repeatedly stressing the lack of a clearly articulated American strategy.
- He sees regime collapse as possible but not yet organized into any clearly viable alternative.
- His view of Iranian attacks on neighbors is that they may be tactically understandable but strategically self-defeating.
- He broadens the conversation from Iran itself to the strain this places on overall U.S. great-power diplomacy.
