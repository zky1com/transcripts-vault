## Summary

Mehmet Oz says the administration's most-favored-nation drug-pricing effort is meant to stop Americans from paying far more than patients in other countries for the same medicines while still preserving innovation. He argues the current approach is not price control because companies can still set the price, but whatever they charge in the U.S. should be matched abroad, and he says Europe must bear more of the cost of sustaining pharmaceutical innovation instead of relying on the U.S. market to do so alone. He also emphasizes that CMS wants to pay more quickly for breakthrough therapies, including gene-editing treatments, when they generate transformative outcomes.

## Key Insights

- Oz frames pricing reform as a burden-sharing correction rather than an anti-pharma attack.
- He repeatedly tries to balance affordability with a strong explicit defense of medical innovation.
- His comments suggest CMS wants to be faster and more flexible on reimbursing true breakthrough therapies.
- He treats Europe as the main free rider in the current global pharmaceutical pricing system.
