## Summary

Doug Burgum argues that U.S. energy security is strongest when the federal government accelerates leasing and permitting so private companies can respond to price signals with more production. He says American producers are likely to raise output amid current supply risks and frames that domestic capacity as a strategic shield against Iranian attempts to disrupt global energy flows and hostage the world economy.

## Key Insights

- Burgum presents energy abundance as both an economic and geopolitical strategy rather than just a commodity policy.
- He emphasizes the federal government's role as a landlord and permitting gatekeeper rather than as the direct producer.
- His argument treats current market volatility as reinforcing the case for domestic capacity, not weakening it.
- He also uses the Iran conflict to justify a broader Western Hemisphere energy strategy.
