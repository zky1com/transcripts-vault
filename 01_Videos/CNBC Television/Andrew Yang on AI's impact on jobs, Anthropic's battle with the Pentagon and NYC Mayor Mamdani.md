## Summary

Andrew Yang argues that AI is moving fast enough to break the old assumption that technological transitions naturally create replacement jobs quickly enough for displaced workers. He says entry-level white-collar work is already under pressure, believes the next year will make the shock much more visible, and argues that policy should shift away from taxing labor toward taxing the AI systems or firms replacing human work.

## Key Insights

- Yang treats the current AI wave as faster and more labor-disruptive than prior automation transitions.
- He sees early warning signs in the collapse of recent computer-science hiring and in executive willingness to cut layers of labor that markets immediately reward.
- His policy instinct is to protect labor by changing the tax system rather than by trying to slow frontier model development directly.
- He also thinks Anthropic's legal position against the Pentagon is strong because the government's arguments are internally inconsistent.
