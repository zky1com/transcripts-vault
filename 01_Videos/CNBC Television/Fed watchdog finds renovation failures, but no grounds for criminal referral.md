The Federal Reserve's Office of the Inspector General has just published its independent review of the Fed's building renovations, and it finds no evidence of criminal wrongdoing or of administrative misconduct among the Fed's Board of Governors in this project. What it does detail is a litany of management failures and bureaucratic issues that allowed costs of the renovations to escalate, and it puts the blame for those broadly on the Fed's board. But no individual Fed officials, including former Chair Jerome Powell, are mentioned by name in the report. Now, here's the IG's key finding. Quote, "While our report outlines numerous deficiencies in the management of the renovation project resulting in our recommended corrective actions, we did not find reasonable grounds to believe that a violation of federal criminal law had occurred and did not identify violations of board policy warranting an administrative misconduct finding." Now, remember, Kelly Powell requested this independent review back in July 2025. That was after the building renovations had become a political flash point. President Trump and his administration were accusing the Fed of mismanagement, questioning whether the project violated federal planning rules and accusing Powell of deceptive congressional testimony. Now, the report does not directly address perjury claims that was not specifically part of the IG's mandate, but the IG did look at documents related to the testimony as part of this review, and there was no misconduct found, criminal or administrative. Looking at the numbers, the IG highlights the Fed board estimated in 2020 that the project would cost $1.3 billion, but as of 2026, the board approved budget had soared to $2.4 billion. It puts the blame for the cost overruns partly on inflation, as the Fed board has said, but it also blamed the board for not asking for a guaranteed maximum price for the project, not asking for more contractor bids for certain aspects, for significant design changes, and for not clearly allocating responsibilities for monitoring the progress. The report then closes out with a response from now Fed Chairman Kevin Worsh. It stated yesterday. Worsh outlines several steps that the Fed will be taking to follow the IG's recommendations. Those include naming the General Services Administration, the new project executive, reviewing contracts to see whether services were fully received and if there are any areas for reimbursement, and then setting clear internal controls around budgeting and oversight for the future. and Kelly formerly the Federal Reserve declines to comment.
