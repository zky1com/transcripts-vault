## Summary

Helima Croft says the market's relief around the Strait of Hormuz depends too heavily on a headline about sovereign insurance guarantees and still lacks the concrete details needed to convince shipping companies and insurers to move normally through the chokepoint. She also argues China may be better positioned than others to endure a long oil disruption because it has been aggressively stockpiling commodities, and she suggests Beijing may care less about immediate Iranian disruption than about the broader strategic balance created when the U.S. gets tied down in the region.

## Key Insights

- Croft treats the market reaction as headline-driven rather than rooted in verified implementation of a shipping solution.
- She highlights LNG and Qatari infrastructure as part of the energy shock, not just crude flows through Hormuz.
- Her China view blends commodity strategy with geopolitics rather than treating stockpiling as a purely commercial move.
- She also underscores the contrast between China's stockpile buildup and the U.S. failure to replenish its own strategic reserves.
