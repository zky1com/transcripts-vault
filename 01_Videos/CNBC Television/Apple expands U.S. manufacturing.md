## Summary

Mackenzie Sigalos reports that Apple is expanding its U.S. manufacturing program by adding Bosch, Cirrus Logic, TDK, and Electronics to its domestic supply chain and investing another $400 million through 2030. She frames the move as part of Tim Cook's broader strategy to shift more upstream production inputs onto U.S. soil while keeping final assembly diversified abroad and absorbing tariff costs rather than immediately passing them through to consumers.

## Key Insights

- Sigalos presents the announcement as an upstream supply-chain expansion rather than a reshoring of final assembly.
- The new partners are tied to specific parts categories like sensors, semiconductor inputs, and motion-related chips.
- She connects the move to Apple's wider balancing act between U.S. political pressure, tariff costs, and diversification into India and Vietnam.
- The segment treats Apple's U.S. manufacturing push as a long-term capital allocation program extending through 2030.
