## Summary

This short note is a compact software-stock drawdown analysis. Drew Cohen’s point is that fears about AI lowering software creation costs do not automatically destroy every incumbent, especially when the customer base is smaller businesses that still need trust, workflow fit, and practical solutions rather than raw code generation.

## Key Insights

- AI making software easier to build does not instantly erase the value of existing software businesses.
- Small-business customers often optimize for trust and usability rather than technical novelty.
- A sharp stock drop can reflect narrative compression more than business destruction.

This stock is down 50%. And so you probably think that the financials are just a dumpster fire right now. Well, you'd be wrong. In fact, they've been growing every single year and free cash flow has grown 150% in the past 3 years. The business is Constellation Software and they own over a thousand vertical market software companies. These are very niche software applications that serve all sorts of small end markets, things you would never think about. Think about software for chicken coops or cemeteries or dentist office. All sorts of very small different applications. And because they're so niche, the markets are not that big and so there's not a lot of competition. Once someone gets embedded in the business, it's very unlikely they ever switch because it's missionritical and runs their entire business. And if they were to switch, they would lose revenue, they could lose customers, they could lose data, and there's just no benefit to it, too, because it is such a small portion of their overall expenses. And so that is why it's very unlikely that these businesses ever change software providers. Right now, there's a lot of fear that AI is going to make software really easy to make, but these small businesses are not going to make software themselves. In fact, how many people have ever tried to make Excel themselves or Microsoft Word? This is not something that people will practically do because it's not that much of a cost savings relative to how much value you get out of it. Plus the peace of mind knowing that it actually works and there's a whole surface department behind it in case something doesn't work. That is really one of the key aspects because just because if you make a product, it doesn't mean people are ever going to use it or even know about it, which is one of the harder aspects of building a software business. And so these are just some of the reasons why Constellation Software is not in as bad of a position as some people may think, despite the fact that their stock is sold off 50% and it trades at 20 times free cash flow.
