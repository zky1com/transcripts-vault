## Summary

Jim Bianco argues that the Treasury selloff is being driven primarily by tariff-related inflation fears rather than by benign inflation prints or narrow technical distortions. He says the market has repriced because investors can no longer assume the Fed will rescue risk assets quickly, and he emphasizes that lasting relief in long yields will require policies the bond market trusts rather than rhetoric from policymakers.

## Key Insights

- The bond market is reacting more to forward inflation risk from tariffs than to backward-looking CPI or PPI relief.
- Technical unwind stories may matter, but Bianco treats them as accelerants rather than the root cause of the move.
- Investors are relearning that the Fed may not cut quickly when inflation concerns dominate.
- A real Fed rescue would likely require much worse market stress than what had appeared so far.
- Easing policy into an inflation scare can perversely push long yields even higher.
- The bond market behaves like its own governing force and cannot be willed lower by officials.
- Lower yields require disinflationary and credibility-enhancing policy, not just stated intentions.
- Potential Chinese selling pressure can show up indirectly through European entities rather than only through direct reserve headlines.
