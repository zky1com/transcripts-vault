# Andrew Ross Sorkin - Democratized investing becomes dangerous when easy leverage arrives before real risk controls

## Position
Andrew Ross Sorkin argues that widening market access is not the problem by itself; the danger comes when cheap leverage spreads faster than risk discipline.

## Evidence Cited
- Transcript says early consumer credit and brokerage margin transformed investing behavior.
- He describes people putting down a dollar and borrowing ten with no meaningful risk underwriting.
- He frames that combination as central to the 1929 setup.

## Logic Chain
1. Democratized finance expands participation.
2. Excess leverage magnifies the downside when underwriting is weak.
3. So the dangerous condition is broad access plus easy leverage without controls.
