# Steve Eisman - Panic selling often leads to a second mistake because investors struggle to buy back in

## Position
Steve Eisman says panic selling is dangerous not only because of the sale itself but because emotion makes re-entry hard.

## Evidence Cited
- Transcript says people who liquidate often cannot buy back in when the market turns.
- He describes the emotional residue of panic decisions.
- He treats that emotional hangover as part of the investing risk.

## Logic Chain
1. Panic selling is driven by fear rather than process.
2. That same fear tends to remain when markets begin recovering.
3. So panic selling often creates a second error: failure to re-enter in time.
